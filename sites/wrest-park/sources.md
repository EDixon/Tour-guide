## County history and archives
- **Victoria County History, Bedford vol. 2 (1908), "Flitton cum Silsoe", pp. 325–333** [39]: the descent of the manor of Wrest from Domesday onwards. Domesday: held by Hugh of Walter, brother of Seier, 4 hides; before 1066 by Leofnoth, a thegn. Held under the barony of Wahull. *(search)* This is the backbone of the ownership chronology.
- Bedfordshire Archives community pages (Silsoe):
  - The Manor of Wrest: the descent, with the barony of Wahull as overlord until 1623 *(search)*
  - Buildings at Wrest Park [10]
  - Monuments and Ornaments at Wrest Park [11]
  - Statues at Wrest Park [38]: the six marble statues round the sundial, probably about 1840 *(search)*
  - Timeline of Events in Silsoe
- Bedfordshire Historical Record Society, *Inventories of Bedfordshire Country Houses 1714–1830*, Wrest Park chapter (Cambridge University Press): Richard, 3rd Earl of Kent, sold Wrest to Sir Henry Wyatt in 1507, and his half-brother Henry bought it back in 1512. *(search)* This is the only source found for those two dates.
- History of Parliament, Sir Henry Grey (about 1583–1639), later 8th Earl of Kent, of Wrest Park.

## Where sources disagree (flag in the text)
- **Column finial:** Bedfordshire Archives says it is a pineapple; Historic England says acanthus. A photo on the visit would settle it.
- **Sale to the Ministry of Works:** 1946 (Historic England timeline, Bedfordshire Archives "about 1946") or 1949 (one blog). The chronology uses 1946.
- **National Institute of Agricultural Engineering arrival:** 1948 (English Heritage) or 1947 (one timeline). The tour uses 1948.
- **Sun Insurance:** bought the estate in 1939 (owners row). The tour's timeline event says "1940, Sun Insurance offices". Both can be true (bought 1939, staff moved in 1939–1940); the science batch should settle it.
- **Silsoe Research Institute closure:** 2006 (English Heritage) or 2007 (Wikipedia, Silsoe).
- **1917 buyer:** named as John George Murray, variously described as a businessman from Thirsk, a manufacturer, or a wealthy industrialist. The current dates file says "brewing and coal businessman", which no source found so far supports.
- **Henry Grey, 4th Earl, and Wrest:** sources differ on whether he bought back all of Wrest (Wikipedia, Wrest Park) or only parts of the estate (his own Wikipedia article, the inventories chapter).
- **Barony numbering:** Nan Herbert is "10th Baroness Lucas" in most sources but "9th" in some.
- **Ruthin settlement:** 1311 or 1319 for the settlement on Roger de Grey. The owners row uses 1311 for Wrest (Victoria County History *(search)*).

## Positions (survey, 12 October 2026)
Checked against OpenStreetMap (`sites/wrest-park/osm-pois.json`, fetched 11 October 2026) and the 2024 leaflet. Moves over about 20 m:
- **Visitor centre (`vc`):** about 110 m. Moved from the edge of the car park to the visitor centre range (café and shop in OSM), which matches the leaflet.
- **Admissions (`entrance`, new):** at the west end of the visitor centre range by the gateway from the car park (leaflet marker 1; OSM toilets and gate). Placed by eye from the leaflet and OSM, so about ±15 m.
- **Car parks (new):** main and overflow, from OSM (named EH car parks).
- **Toilets south of the walled garden (new):** OSM toilet building, shown on the leaflet.
- **French Parterre:** about 90 m. Moved from well south of the parterre to its centre, where OSM's "French Garden" and its four statues are.
- **Garden front and terrace:** about 60 m. Moved onto the terrace walk on the main axis, at the top of the steps down to the parterre (OSM steps).
- **Head of the Long Water:** about 145 m. OSM's Long Water runs from 52.0009 to 52.0039 N, and a Commons photo cluster sits at its north end. The old position was on the lawn by the sundial, which is now its own stop. The `longwater` bullet "Look for Mercury and Cupid on the east bank, about 70 m from this end" must be checked against the new position.
- **Bath House:** about 30 m. Moved to the OSM building.
- **Science years:** about 70 m. Moved inside the garden to the east end of the cross walk, looking north-east to the former research campus (it was outside the site boundary).
- **Chinese Temple and Bridge:** 34 m from the OSM temple. Left between the temple and the bridge on purpose.
- Others agree with OSM within 15 m: Orangery, Bowling Green House, Pavilion, both Half Houses, Brown Column, altars, Atlas, William III, Mithraic Altar, Italian Garden.
