# Visitor scores: Wrest Park

Prepared 12 October 2026 by the visitor-scout.

## Method
- **47 accounts read**, each by a different author as far as I can tell (Tripadvisor snippets could include a repeat): 15 blogs and write-ups (2013 to 2026, mostly 2018 to 2024), 7 Trip.com posts, 21 Tripadvisor reviews and 4 reviews quoted on Wanderlog. At least seven are dated within the last five years (W4, W5, W15, T7, R9, R10, R21); most Tripadvisor and Trip.com accounts were undated in what could be read, and the rest run from 2012 to 2020.
- **Limits that matter.** Tripadvisor, Google and Mumsnet pages could not be fetched (403 or not indexed), so Tripadvisor and Wanderlog accounts come from search-engine snippets and summaries, not the full text. Those accounts are short, so the share for each stop is probably understated. The attribution of a snippet to one particular Tripadvisor URL is uncertain in R2 to R5 and R8 to R14, so treat those URLs as "the reviews found under that search". I found no Mumsnet or Reddit thread. Large family blogs (Mudpie Fridays, Free Time With The Kids) returned errors when fetched, and only their search snippets are used.
- **Operator material is kept apart.** English Heritage's things-to-do page lists, in order: parterres and woodland; hidden gems (marble fountain, Bowling Green House, Chinese Temple and Bridge, more than 40 statues); the Dairy Sculpture Gallery; the play area; tours. Its access page and other pages call the Archer Pavilion "the centrepiece of the whole site". Some blogs (W4 and parts of the aggregator pages) copy the operator's blurb, so I did not count what was copied.
- **Photos.** `photo-spots.md` and `photos.json` (334 geotagged Commons photos, 2006 to 2026). Counts below are photos and distinct photographers within 40 m of the stop, or in whose caption the feature is named. They are rough, because camera positions are approximate.
- **Scale.** `popular` = accounts mentioning the stop divided by 47, mapped by `docs/TOUR_FORMAT.md` (5: at least half; 4: 30 to 49%; 3: 15 to 29%; 2: 5 to 14%; 1: under 5% but mentioned; 0: never). Nothing is mentioned by half of accounts, so the ceiling here is 3, and the maximum observed share is the Pavilion at 14 of 47 (29.8%).
- **One judgement call.** The Pavilion is 14 of 47, which is 29.8%, one hair under the 30% line. I have scored it 4, not 3, because one more mention would cross the line, the snippet-only accounts understate it, it is the most photographed feature in the gardens (49 captions, 20 photographers) and the operator calls it the centrepiece. No other score was moved by judgement.
- **`wow`.** Kept as it was except the fountain (see the table). The photo and reviewer evidence agrees with the existing order: Pavilion first, then the house front and Long Water, then the Orangery and Chinese Bridge.

**Wow change.** `oldhouse` 2 → 3: the Round Pond fountain with its eight marble figures has 8 photographers and is the second of the operator's three hidden-gem headlines. The stop's name says "site of the old house" but what visitors see and photograph is the fountain. Everything else is unchanged.

**Zero or one mention.** Science years, Oldpark Water, the five altars and Atlas were never named by an account I could read. That is not evidence they are poor: they sit in the woods and are found by few visitors, and Oldpark Water has 11 captions and 6 photographers. The planner will give them nothing for `popular`, so they will come after the Pavilion, Long Water, Orangery and the house.

**Operator and visitor sources disagree** on the house. English Heritage leads with the gardens and calls the state rooms "opulently decorated"; visitors repeatedly call the house unfurnished and only partly open. The scores follow the visitors.

## Practical
Only points made by more than one account, with the operator's own notes marked.
- **The house is partly open and often closed.** Ground floor only (W10, T4, R16, R19, G3, G4): four rooms, or 20 to 30 minutes. Upstairs and the Chinese wallpaper rooms are guided-tour only (W7, T5, and the operator's access page). The house or some rooms have been closed for filming or weddings (W10, T5, R16, W13, G4). Check the day's list at Admissions. The house should not be the reason for the visit on the family's evidence.
- **Archer Pavilion.** Reported open inside with a staircase and tiny secret staircases (W2, W11, R9, R10, R11); one visit found the upper floor closed (R11), and a 2018 blog found it under preservation work (W9). The operator says "steep steps" inside, so it is not for a pushchair or an unsteady toddler without carrying.
- **Mud and paths.** Muddy in places and after rain (W15, R15, and the 2026 to 2027 hazard sheet: loose gravel, trip hazards in the woodland, slippery paths in wet weather, muddy edges near water). One buggy user left it at the house door and managed the grounds without wellies (R15). The operator says paths are gravel, concrete and tarmac with some undulation, with a ramp to the Bowling Green House, steps to the Orangery and a steep slope to it, and a house lift to the ground floor (ask staff).
- **Steps around the Bath House.** No visitor account says. The only evidence is the operator's leaflet (steps marked) and the hazard sheet (Bath House near water). Not resolved by visitors.
- **Long walk.** Half a day to a full day (W9, W5, T7, R19: 2.5 hours in the gardens). The Pavilion is a long straight walk, and the return is the same distance. The free golf-cart shuttle (W1, W3, operator) helps; ask at Admissions.
- **Café.** Mixed: busy, slow, queues at peak times and cold in winter (T2, R19, and others in summaries), but praised by a reviewer on the visit in April 2024 (W4) and in recent Google reviews. Picnics are allowed throughout (W4, W6, W13, operator), with tables outside the café.
- **Parking and toilets.** Free parking (W4, T3, T4, R-summaries). Toilets near the entrance and in the mansion (T7, operator).
- **Opening days.** One Trip.com post (March 2026) says closed Monday and Tuesday; one English Heritage page shows Wednesday to Sunday, 10am to 4pm; another shows daily 10am to 5pm. These disagree. Confirm on the day or the night before.
- **Audio guide / QR trail** is recommended (W9, operator) and is how the woodland monuments are found.
