# Karnak: visitor scores

No internal or confidential material (and nothing about Roboverse Reply finances) was used. All sources are public web pages.

## Method
- **23 independent visitor accounts** read between 22 November 2026 and today: 18 blog or write-up authors (each counted once) and 5 separate Google reviews shown on a Wanderlog page. Dates run from 2011 to July 2026. Only 13 are from 2023 to 2026; the rest are older (2018 to 2022, and one from 2011) and are marked in the log. Several accounts name no stop at all; they stay in the denominator because they still carry practical evidence.
- **Tripadvisor refused WebFetch (403)** and search snippets gave no usable individual reviews, so Tripadvisor is not in the count. Three further blogs also returned 403. Lonely Planet and the Egyptian Ministry page also refused. The Wikimedia Commons category could not be reached from here, so the photo evidence uses `sites/karnak/photos.json` and `photo-spots.md` only.
- **Not counted as visitor accounts:** Rough Guides, Lonely Planet (via search snippets), WeRoad and a tour-company route guide (guides, G1 to G3 in the log), and commercial pages. They are used for the operator-style "highlights" notes and for practical points.
- A mention is matched to a stop only when the name or the description is unmistakable. Judgement calls: "the fallen or broken Hatshepsut obelisk near the Sacred Lake" counts for `scarab` (it is part of that stop); "the Second Pylon" or "the colossus of Ramesses II with his daughter" counts for `pylon2`; Khonsu named only as a god in a barque-shrine room does not count for `khonsu`; "columns" with no hall named does not count for `hypostyle`.
- **Weak spot in the evidence:** most of these blogs follow the standard route (rams, pylon, hall, obelisks, lake), so stops off that route (northwall, bubastite, mkcourt, east, quay) score 0 or 1 even if good. Treat `popular` for those as "rarely singled out", not "poor".
- Photo counts are geotagged Commons files within 60 m of a stop, from `photos.json` (1,340 photos). Geotags are rough, and the Taharqa position sits in the middle of the forecourt, so it also catches the Sety II shrine, Ramesses III temple and Bubastite Portal. Use them as a hint only.

Operator "highlights": the Ministry page (egymonuments.gov.eg) refused WebFetch, so the operator list was not read. ### Suggested `mustSee`
Currently set: rams, hypostyle, obelisks. Evidence supports keeping those and adding **lake** (39%, and the one place reviewers call atmospheric) and **scarab** only if the planner wants a toddler-friendly beetle near the lake; do not add pylon1 (wow 5 but 17%) unless it is already on the route anyway. Akhmenu is the best-supported extra for looks but its open rooms are limited.

## Not a stop yet
- **Sound and Light show.** Named by three accounts as an evening extra (A4 skipped it over mixed reports, A11 skipped it, A19 attended with a baby in a carrier who fussed during the narration). Separate evening ticket. Not a stop; the family arrives at 08:30. No.
- **The Avenue of Sphinxes to Luxor Temple.** Mentioned as a side note by A1, A8 and guides; it is outside the ticketed precinct. No.
- **Hatshepsut's "Wall of Records" and Third to Sixth Pylons.** Named in one route list (A2). The first is part of the obelisks/sanctuary area. Could be a `look` line at `obelisks` or `sanctuary`, not a stop.
- **Coptic saints painted over the columns.** Two accounts single out the Christian paintings (A5, A9) and one the Egyptian colour on ceilings. Worth a line in the `akhmenu` or hall bullets if the researcher can place the paintings; not a stop.
- **Sunrise at the far (east) end.** One account (A5) had the site almost to themselves at 06:00 to 06:20 in November. Not relevant for an 08:30 arrival.
- **Temple of Mut and Montu precincts.** Outside scope; separate ticket; Montu closed (A15).
- **Colossi of Amun and Amunet** (A15 found them under a tarpaulin). No.

## Practical
Only points made by more than one account are listed as findings. Single-account items are marked.

- **Crowds by time of day.** The consistent picture is early is quiet, then busy. Tour buses from the Red Sea arrive from about 09:00 to 10:00 and the main courts are busy until about 16:00 (A2, A7, A18, G3, G4). One writer arrived at 08:15 and found it quiet, with buses arriving before they left at 10:00 (A18). Another found it "very crowded" by 08:30 in November (A5). Other reviewers: "very busy" in January 2026 (G-review). Guides usually start at 09:00 (G1). **Expect an 08:30 arrival to be the tail of the quiet period; do the Hypostyle Hall and the forecourt first, then go to the quieter outlying stops (Khonsu, Open Air Museum, Akhmenu, lake) while the buses build.** A7 says Khonsu stayed peaceful and A18 and A6 say the Open Air Museum and Akhmenu were quieter than the hall. Nobody wrote about Ramadan crowds at Karnak. Planning guides say Ramadan hours may shift slightly but monuments stay open (G5), so treat the 06:00 to 17:00 opening as likely but unconfirmed for early March 2027. Eid al-Fitr is expected just after the end of Ramadan (around 9 to 10 March 2027), so early March should fall just before it.
- **Heat and shade.** Little shade outside the Hypostyle Hall; the rams' avenue has none (A2, A6, A7, G4, G5). All March figures are secondary: Rough Guides calls March and April "pleasant" with fewer crowds (G1); no account reports a March visit. Heat complaints come from May to July (+45 degrees Celsius in May, G-review July 2026; "over 100 degrees" in July, A5). Hat that covers the neck and water for a child in a carrier.
- **Touts and hassle.** Four accounts report touts offering "secret" or "photo spots" for a tip (A4, A6, A7, A13), and one reports vendors working the entrance and car park (A6). A "la shukran" and a steady walk is the universal answer. A15 was quoted 200 EGP for a Luxor Pass and haggled it to 50 EGP, describing it as a scam; avoid buying the Luxor Pass at the desk. Single report: two of the guides advise avoiding unofficial guides who offer access to "restricted areas" (G4).
- **Ticketing, card or cash: the sources disagree.** Card only: A7 ("several ticket offices are card-only"), G4 (Visa or Mastercard only, paper refused, outages possible, 2025 or 2026), the Departly page (card-only since 2026). Cash: A6 ("tickets at the gate with cash, as credit cards aren't accepted", July 2026), a guide page costed at 1 November 2026 (paid in Egyptian pounds), the Explore Luxor FAQ ("the main Karnak counter accepts cards; cash is safest"). **Carry both a contactless card and about 1,500 EGP in small notes.** Price 600 EGP adult foreign (A7, A12, A18, G4); under-6s free (A4, 2022 price list), so the two-year-old should be free but the age rule comes from a 2022 account. Open Air Museum is included in the ticket in recent accounts (A7, A18); older guides (G1 and G2) charged extra.
- **Toilets and café.** Single account only (A6, July 2026): public toilets cost 5 EGP, cleaned once a day, take your own paper and sanitiser. Two guides say toilets, kiosks and souvenir shops are near the entrance but services inside are limited (G5, a commercial guide). No visitor describes the café by the lake or the toilets at the Open Air Museum or east of the lake. Treat the in-site facilities as uncertain; use the visitor-centre toilets before entering.
- **Carrier, not a pushchair.** Two sources say uneven ground makes pushchairs impractical and carriers are better (Explore Luxor, a family blog from 2011); AAA says the site is pushchair-accessible with bumpy paths. A19 had a toddler happy in a carrier; switching it from back to front calmed him. Steps up to the White Chapel and Khonsu stairs are fine for the family.
- **Timing and length.** Two hours is the commonest figure including the Open Air Museum (A18, A4), three if you like detail. A2, A5 and A7 recommend a guide to explain what you are looking at; A1's guided group had only 20 minutes and said it was nowhere near enough.
- **Closures reported in recent accounts (dates are of the write-up, not necessarily the visit):**
  - **Khonsu:** much of the interior roped off; a guard gave access to the roof for a small tip (A15, written 2020, updated 2024). A7 and A12 describe going in and seeing the ceiling, so the Khonsu interior state is uncertain; plan to see it from the doorway and the colonnade.
  - **Opet temple:** locked, seen only through side screens (A15).
  - **Ptah:** a room locked until a guard opened it (A15); another reviewer saw the Sekhmet statue (A15 only). The Sekhmet is reachable but may need a tip.
  - **Southern pylons:** "most of the area beyond the Eighth Pylon is closed" and "eastern and southern gates were locked" (A7, A15).
  - **Akhmenu:** "few rooms in the Festival Hall are open" (A7). The southern chapels were reported restored and opened to the public in June 2025 (not a visitor account; ancientpages.com).
  - **Open Air Museum:** no closure reported by anyone; included in the ticket.
  - **Fallen Hatshepsut obelisk roped off** (A15), and the Amun and Amunet colossi covered by a tarpaulin (A15). The Montu precinct was off limits (A15).
  - No account is from March 2027 or from the last six months for these specific closures, so all of the above should be treated as unconfirmed.
