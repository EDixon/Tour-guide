# Karnak: research plan

Survey of 22 November 2026. New site. Sources are listed in `sites/karnak/sources.md` (several are search results only, marked *(search)*: open them and confirm before relying on them). Dated things and people are drafted in `knowledge/karnak/dates.json`; use those ids, add `who`, `events` and `related`, and correct dates.

General rules for every batch:
- **Chronology.** Years BC are negative in `dates.json`. Reign dates follow Shaw (ed.), *The Oxford History of Ancient Egypt* (2000). Where a source uses another chronology, convert to Shaw's or say which is used. In text write "about 1457 BC", never "c.1457".
- **Site-specific sources.** Every claim about Karnak itself needs a Karnak source: Digital Karnak, the UCLA Encyclopedia of Egyptology, CFEETK publications, the Great Hypostyle Hall Project, Blyth (2006), Porter and Moss, peer-reviewed papers. Wikipedia only as a pointer.
- **The site board.** Eliot's photo of the board "Karnak Temples Plan & Chronology" is cited as source 9. Where it disagrees with scholarship (e.g. "Chapels of Sety I"), give the scholarly answer and say the board differs.
- **Access.** For every stop, find the most recent evidence (2024–2026 reviews, CFEETK news) of whether it is open to visitors. Where it stays uncertain, write the `note` so the app says so ("may be closed; if so, see it from …"). Never "check on site".
- **Toddler tasks:** two or three `kids` per stop that a two-year-old can genuinely find (an animal in a relief, a big beetle, a giant foot), each with a photo from `sites/karnak/photos.json` where one shows it.
- **Timeline `more`:** 1–3 sentences, under 60 words, for each event listed against the batch. Use the year and label exactly as given.
- **No Habsburg angle** unless a real one turns up (none expected).

## Batch 1: Arrival, the river and the First Pylon
- **Stops:** quay, rams, pylon1, ramp
- **Ids:** quay, rams-avenue, first-pylon, builders-ramp, sphinx-avenue-reopening; people nectanebo-i, ramesses-ii, pinedjem-i, sety-ii
- **Start from:** Digital Karnak (First Pylon, quay, ramp, "architectural features" PDF); Pennington and others in *Antiquity* (the shifting Nile and Karnak's origins); Ghilardi and Boraik on the western quay; Eliot's geoarchaeology plan (source unknown; see "Identify the geoarchaeology plan" below); Arnold, *Building in Egypt* (1991) on construction ramps; PBS 2021 on the Avenue of Sphinxes.
- **Angles:**
  - engineering: how a pylon was built (rubble core, casing blocks, ramps of mud brick), what the unfinished First Pylon shows (undressed faces, the ramp left in place), its size
  - geography and physics: the Nile has moved west since antiquity; how the quay and the rams' avenue relate to the old river; flood levels marked on the quay
  - the rams: Amun's sacred animal; whose names they carry (Ramesses II, then Pinedjem); the 2.7 km avenue to Luxor Temple and its 2021 reopening
- **Check:** the First Pylon's date (Nectanebo I, or later), its width and height; who built the rams (moved here from elsewhere?); the Sety II obelisk and stele by the quay.
- **Identify the geoarchaeology plan (Eliot's choice, 22 November 2026).** Eliot can't remember where he saved it. It is a black-and-white plan of the Amun-Ra, Montu and Mut precincts on a modern street map, with core soundings labelled AS01 to AS40 and DS1, profiles P1 to P15, and these labels: "Tribune of the temple of Montu", "Roman bath", "Ptolemaic bath", "Saite quay" (twice), "Western tribune", "Ramps", "Neferhotep's temple", "Double curved quay (of Montuemhat ?)", "Gate of Ptolemy XII", "Eastern quay of an island", "Dromos of Nectanebo", "Gate of Hatshepsut". Search the Karnak palaeo-channel papers (Graham, Pennington, Strutt, Ghilardi, Boraik and others, about 2012–2020; *Antiquity*, *Journal of Archaeological Science*, *Géomorphologie*, CFEETK's *Cahiers de Karnak*) for a figure that matches. If found, cite that paper (with figure number) in `sources.md` and use it for the river story. If not, don't cite the plan at all; use the published papers directly and record "plan not identified" in STATUS.
- **Events:** -370 "Nectanebo I's walls and the First Pylon"; 2021 "Avenue of Sphinxes reopens to Luxor"

## Batch 2: The great forecourt
- **Stops:** sety2, taharqa, ram3, bubastite
- **Ids:** sety-ii-shrine, taharqa-kiosk, ramesses-iii-temple, bubastite-portal; people sety-ii, taharqa, ramesses-iii, shoshenq-i
- **Start from:** Digital Karnak; Porter and Moss II; Blyth chapters 12–14; Oriental Institute (Chicago), *Reliefs and Inscriptions at Karnak* vols. I–III (Ramesses III's temple; the Bubastite Portal).
- **Angles:**

  - Ramesses III's temple as a complete small temple, a model for the parts of Karnak that are ruined
- **Check:** Sety I or Sety II for the triple shrine (the board says Sety I); whether the forecourt was roofed in any phase.
- **Events:** -1184 "Ramesses III's temple in the forecourt"; -925 "Shoshenq I's campaign relief"; -690 "Taharqa's kiosk"

## Batch 3: The Great Hypostyle Hall
- **Stops:** pylon2, hypostyle, northwall
- **Ids:** second-pylon-colossus, hypostyle-hall, sety-battle-reliefs, hypostyle-collapse; people sety-i, ramesses-ii, ramesses-i (add), horemheb (add), georges-legrain, pinedjem-i
- **Start from:** Great Hypostyle Hall Project (Brand; architecture, reliefs, early recording and restoration pages); Digital Karnak (Hypostyle Hall, clerestory lighting); Legrain's reports in *Annales du Service* (1899–1902); Wikipedia only as a pointer.
- **Angles (high interest):**
  - engineering: how the columns were built (half-drums, sandstone from Gebel el-Silsila), how the roof slabs and architraves were lifted, the foundations (small, poor blocks: why columns fell)
  - physics: the clerestory window grilles and light; why the central columns are taller
  - the 3 October 1899 collapse of eleven columns (groundwater and salt), Legrain's re-erection to May 1902, and the modern cleaning that revealed the paint
  - computing: Digital Karnak's 3D model of the hall's phases
  - history: Sety I's campaigns on the north wall; Ramesses II's Kadesh battle on the south wall
- **Check:** column heights (sources give 20/14, 21/10, 24/14 m), the floor area, whether Horemheb or Ramesses I began it; who the colossus is (Ramesses II usurped by Pinedjem).
- **Events:** -1294 "Sety I builds the Hypostyle Hall"; -1290 "Sety I's battle reliefs"; 1899 "Eleven Hypostyle columns collapse"

## Batch 4: Obelisks and the sanctuary
- **Stops:** obelisks, scarab, sanctuary
- **Ids:** thutmose-i-obelisk, hatshepsut-obelisk, fallen-obelisk, third-pylon, scarab, sanctuary; people thutmose-i, hatshepsut, thutmose-iii, amenhotep-iii, philip-arrhidaeus
- **Start from:** Digital Karnak (obelisks; Wadjet Hall; Third Pylon; bark shrine of Philip Arrhidaeus); Blyth chapters 4–6; Habachi, *The Obelisks of Egypt* (1977); Engelbach on the Aswan unfinished obelisk; Hatshepsut's Deir el-Bahari reliefs of obelisk transport.
- **Angles (high interest):**
  - engineering: quarrying granite at Aswan (dolerite pounders), moving an obelisk of several hundred tonnes by barge, raising it (the competing theories; say they are theories)
  - Hatshepsut's inscription that her obelisks took seven months to quarry, and the electrum caps
  - why Thutmose III walled her obelisk in, and how that preserved it
  - the heraldic granite pillars (lotus and papyrus: Upper and Lower Egypt)
  - the giant scarab (where it came from: Amenhotep III's temple on the west bank?) and the modern walk-round custom
- **Check:** Hatshepsut's obelisk height (29.5 or about 30 m) and weight (323–374 tonnes in the sources); the fallen top's location and which obelisk it is; the scarab's date and origin.
- **Events:** -1500 "Thutmose I's pylons and obelisks"; -1457 "Hatshepsut's obelisks raised"; -1370 "Amenhotep III's Third Pylon and the scarab"; -323 "Granite sanctuary for Philip Arrhidaeus"

## Batch 5: Thutmose III's east end, the oldest core and Ptah
- **Stops:** mkcourt, akhmenu, ptah, east
- **Ids:** senusret-temple, akhmenu, ptah-temple, lateran-obelisk, hearing-ear-temple; people senusret-i, thutmose-iii, ramesses-ii; add constantius-ii and domenico-fontana if used
- **Start from:** Digital Karnak (Akhmenu, Middle Kingdom court, Ptah temple, east temples); Sullivan UEE (2010); Blyth chapters 2 and 6; Beaux, *Le Cabinet de curiosités de Thoutmosis III* (1990) for the botanical garden; Belmonte and others on Karnak's solar alignment; CFEETK on the Ptah temple restoration.
- **Angles:**
  - space and astronomy: the main axis and the winter solstice sunrise (what the evidence is, and the disagreements); the solar chapel on the Akhmenu roof
  - the botanical garden reliefs: plants and animals from Thutmose III's Syrian campaign
  - the tent-pole columns (imitating a festival tent in stone)
  - the Akhmenu as a Christian church: painted saints on the columns
  - the Lateran obelisk: raised for Thutmose III (finished by Thutmose IV), taken to Rome by Constantius II in AD 357, found broken and re-erected in 1588 by Domenico Fontana; the tallest standing ancient obelisk (engineering)
  - the Sekhmet statue in the Ptah temple and the light from the roof
- **Check:** whether the Akhmenu's solar chapel and botanical garden are open; the date the Middle Kingdom temple was dismantled.
- **Events:** -1950 "Senusret I builds the first stone temple"; -1456 "Thutmose III begins the Akhmenu"; 357 "Lateran obelisk shipped to Rome"; 400 "Temple closed; churches in the ruins"

## Batch 7: The Sacred Lake and the south
- **Stops:** lake, lakeview, cachette, southpylons, khonsu (five, but lakeview and southpylons are small)
- **Ids:** sacred-lake, seventh-pylon, hittite-treaty, cachette, khonsu-temple; add the Temple of Opet and the Bab el-Amara gate; people thutmose-iii, ramesses-ii, ramesses-iii, georges-legrain
- **Start from:** Digital Karnak; Legrain's Cachette reports (Heidelberg digitised *Archaeological Report* and *Annales du Service*); the IFAO/CFEETK Cachette database; Oriental Institute, *The Temple of Khonsu* vols. I–II; CFEETK on Opet.
- **Angles:**
  - how the lake is fed (groundwater, the water table) and what it was used for
  - the Cachette: about 750 statues and 17,000 bronzes buried in Ptolemaic times; why; how they were dug out of the water table
  - the Hittite peace treaty on the wall by the Cachette Court: the earliest surviving peace treaty with both sides' versions
  - Khonsu, the moon god, and the Opet temple's crypt
- **Check:** whether the southern axis (Seventh to Tenth Pylons) and the Khonsu and Opet temples are open; the lake's size.
- **Events:** -1259 "Peace treaty with the Hittites recorded"; -1170 "Temple of Khonsu begun"; 1903 "Cachette of statues found (to 1905)"
